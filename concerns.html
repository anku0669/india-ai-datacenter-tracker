⚠️ Concerns

Why This Build-Out Is a Risk for India, Right Now

Risks tied to the current, already-announced pipeline — distinct from the further-scaling risks covered on the Scaling page.

🚱

Water stress in already-scarce regions

With three-quarters of facilities sited in water-stressed districts, and cities like Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Chennai and Thane already running below daily municipal demand, large-scale industrial water withdrawal risks deepening shortages for residents, farmers and small businesses — populations that are disproportionately poorer and less able to absorb the cost of scarcity.

📜

Regulatory and disclosure gaps

India currently has no mandatory water-stress mapping and no standard thermal or environmental-impact assessment requirement specific to data centers — the sector is classified under IT/ITeS rules, exempting it from the environmental clearance process applied to other large infrastructure. Lawyers and activists have flagged the approved 1 GW Vizag Mega Data Centre Park and Google's facility there for failing to disclose water sourcing at the approval stage.

🔌

Grid strain and rising electricity costs

A roughly 6x increase in dedicated data center demand by 2030 will require substantial new generation, transmission and substation capacity concentrated in a handful of states. Without matching grid investment, local communities and industries risk higher tariffs or reduced reliability, echoing utility-rate disputes already seen around U.S. data centers.

🌦️

Weather-timing risk

India's data center expansion is accelerating just as forecasters flag a possible El Niño-driven weak 2026 monsoon — a combination that could sharply increase competition for water precisely when supply is under pressure, with little regulatory infrastructure in place to arbitrate between industrial and public use.

🏘️

Community friction is already visible

In Thane, the WakeUp Thanekars group is demanding a site-specific accounting of Amazon's Balkum facility after officials cited three contradictory water figures. Similar objections have emerged around the Vizag mega-cluster — early signals of the kind of local, town-by-town resistance the Brockovich Data Center project has documented extensively in the U.S.

⚖️

Equity and displacement dimensions

Researchers writing about the Vizag and Bengaluru cases note that communities most exposed to water withdrawal and land acquisition are often lower-income, Dalit, Adivasi and rural populations with the least influence over state-level investment decisions. Land acquisition of several hundred acres per campus raises resettlement, compensation and long-term local employment questions.
