📈 Scaling Up

If India Builds Even More Data Centers: The Cons

The current 12 GW pipeline is not the ceiling — analysts project India could reach 20-30+ GW beyond 2030 if AI demand keeps compounding. Here is what continued, larger-scale expansion specifically risks, on top of the current-pipeline concerns.

🏭

Renewable buildout may not keep pace

If data center demand outgrows new renewable capacity additions, operators and grid operators will lean harder on coal and gas to fill the gap — especially during non-solar hours, since AI workloads run 24/7. That would quietly undo some of the climate gains from India's 50%+ non-fossil capacity milestone.

🌾

Direct competition with agriculture over water

Agriculture already uses ~80% of India's water. Even a small proportional increase in industrial water demand concentrated in specific irrigation-dependent basins could tip local water balances, particularly during weak-monsoon years, directly affecting farmer livelihoods and food security in those districts.

🏚️

Escalating land acquisition and displacement

Scaling from ~10 major hub sites today to dozens more implies proportionally larger and more frequent land acquisitions — likely pushing further into agricultural, coastal, and semi-forested land as the best-connected urban sites are exhausted, raising the scale of displacement and compensation disputes.

📉

Stranded-asset and overbuild risk

Much of the announced pipeline assumes AI compute demand keeps compounding at current rates. If global AI investment cools or shifts to more efficient models needing less raw compute, India could be left with expensive, underutilized gigawatt campuses, dedicated power plants, and desalination infrastructure built on now-outdated demand forecasts.

🌐

Deepening import dependence on chips & hardware

India has limited domestic capacity to manufacture the advanced GPUs and networking silicon these campuses require. A larger build-out means a larger recurring import bill and greater exposure to global chip-supply shocks and export-control policy shifts from chip-producing countries.

🧾

Regulatory scramble outpaced by construction speed

Facilities are being approved and built faster than water-stress mapping, environmental clearance rules, or disclosure standards are being written. Each additional gigawatt built before those rules exist makes retrofitting standards onto already-operating campuses harder and more expensive later.
