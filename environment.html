🌎 Environment

Environmental Concerns Beyond Power & Water

Power and water get most of the attention, but a gigawatt-scale build-out touches land, air, biodiversity and the waste stream too. Each concern below is distinct enough to warrant its own scrutiny.

🌫️

Carbon emissions tied to the grid mix

Coal still supplies 43% of India's installed power capacity. A data center plugged into the public grid without a dedicated renewable-plus-storage contract inherits that carbon intensity. Even with ~50% non-fossil capacity nationally, actual generation still leans on thermal plants during peak and non-solar hours — exactly when 24/7 AI compute needs power most.

🔋

Diesel backup generators: air & noise pollution

Nearly all large data centers run diesel generators for backup power. These emit particulate matter and NOx during testing and outages, and constant cooling-system and generator noise has been a recurring nuisance complaint near U.S. facilities — a pattern regulators expect to repeat in dense Indian metros as campuses scale up.

🏗️

Land use, deforestation & local ecosystems

Individual campuses run 250-935+ acres (Odisha's proposed Adani site, Reliance's Vizag land allocation). At gigawatt scale this means significant conversion of agricultural, coastal or forested land, with associated topsoil loss, habitat fragmentation, and reduced natural groundwater recharge from paved, impervious surfaces.

🐟

Coastal & marine impact from desalination

Desalinated seawater cooling (Jamnagar, planned for Vizag) solves the freshwater problem but creates a new one: concentrated brine discharge back into coastal waters can raise local salinity and harm marine ecosystems if not carefully managed and monitored — a trade-off regulators have not yet built clear rules around.

♻️

E-waste from rapid hardware refresh cycles

AI-optimized GPU hardware is refreshed far more frequently than traditional enterprise IT — often every 2-3 years versus 5-7. A sector scaling from 1.9 GW to 12+ GW implies a proportional surge in decommissioned servers, GPUs and networking gear, and India currently lacks data-center-specific e-waste recycling mandates.

🌡️

Localized heat island effects

Dense clusters of always-on cooling equipment raise ambient temperature in their immediate vicinity, compounding urban heat-island effects already present in metros like Mumbai, Chennai and Hyderabad — a second-order climate burden that falls on nearby residents, not the operator.
